WorldForge 2 — Observed History on the Map

Schematic of how the proposed epochal war, league / hegemony, social-strata & unrest, and Ages timeline layers would look as map overlays. Colours pulled from OverlayManager.ts; new marks proposed additions. Dark theme matches the app. The world stays observed — these are read-outs of an emergent AI history.

1 · An epochal war on the trade map (balanced land + sea)

♛ capital / polis seat city bundled trade trunk sea lane dynamic flow war front / blockade / battle siege ring rogue company fleet token

A war reuses the existing route network. One war can run a naval front (blockaded lanes, fleet tokens, battle markers) and a land front (a contested corridor with a front line that creeps toward the losing capital) at once — so a polis can win the sea and lose the land. A siege ring drains the city's food reserve; an unpaid mercenary company turns rogue and becomes a moving disaster.

2 · Leagues, tribute & a hegemony (the political map that evolves)

Leagues render as dashed membership halos; tribute flows as violet arrows into the hegemon. A dominant league becomes a hegemony (ringed capital) — its bloom grows as it wins epochal wars and shrinks when tributaries revolt.

Hegemonies overreach and collapse — a revolt during a plague or after a lost war shatters the bloc. The map breathes across centuries instead of freezing into one winner.

3 · Social strata & unrest — the city inspector (HubPanel)

A healthy city: bread is cheap, inequality moderate, unrest low. The strata bar shows the population structure; mobility is rising.

Famine + extreme inequality → unrest boils → revolt unseats the dominant council_house and flips the regime (anacyclosis). Production drops, trade routes detour around the city — visible on the main map too.

4 · The Ages timeline scrubber (the observed-mode centrepiece)

The sim segments its own 300-year run into named Ages (auto-titled from each span's dominant events). Dragging the handle replays the political/economic map at that year — borders, hegemonies, trade dominance, war fronts, plague waves all animate. Event ticks are causally linked (cartel squeeze → riot → regime flip → war → default → crash), so the Chronicle reads as cause-and-effect, not a flat log. This is the payoff of an observed world: its history is a readable artifact.
